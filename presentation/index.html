Who has the ball?

Open with the question every analyst asks first: who has the ball? Point at the board: O is Barça, X is Athletic, yellow is the ball. Then explain the bar at the bottom.

One question, asked 199,319 times

The match is cut into 25 still pictures per second. For every one, a program has to say who has the ball:

  • H: Barça, ball in play
  • A: Athletic, ball in play
  • D: dead ball (throw-ins, corners, fouls, half-time)

The whole entry is one file with 199,319 answers, produced by a program. Nobody may fill it in by hand.

Every picture gets one letter. Each uninterrupted home or away spell is a possession. Dead-ball stretches separate play.

Start with the moving dots

It gets 23 dots on a rectangle, 25 times a second: 11 Barça players, 11 Athletic players and the ball.

And the dots are messy:

  • the ball is visible in only 57% of moments
  • sometimes it "teleports" for a split second
  • players at the edge of the picture vanish

Ball visibility: OVERVIEW.md

We see shirts, faces and the referee. The computer sees coordinates from automatic tracking on a TV-style camera, which is why they're noisy.

Why half a second of doubt ruins a whole possession

The judges match each predicted possession to one real possession by overlap in time. They average F1 across five overlap thresholds.

Too jumpy, and one doubt splits a 30-second possession into three: the extra pieces can reduce both precision and recall.

Too smooth, and a real one-second steal disappears, taking three possessions with it.

Scoring: EVALUATION.md and the official scorer

This is the key slide. Per-moment accuracy can be about 95 while the possession score is far lower; that gap is the whole challenge. The skill we need: tell a real change of possession from a touch that doesn't count.

We were given the answers for only 5 minutes

The public sample contains 17 possessions. The organisers scored the full match for the ranking. We needed other matches to learn what a change of possession looks like.

So we built our own exam from 10 full matches with known answers. We held out each Sportec match in turn and checked transfer on Metrica. One calibration step also used the Metrica games.

Main exam: 7 German league matches, learn from 6 and test on the 7th, repeated for each. Broken ball: the same matches with the ball data damaged on purpose. New supplier: 3 matches from Metrica Sports. The public 5 minutes are only a sanity check.

Where we started: whoever is closest wins

The organisers' example gives the ball to the team with the nearest player, then smooths over a couple of seconds.

It fails in exactly the ways from the scoring slide: every pass flips the answer as the ball flies past a rival, it can't tell when the ball is dead, and when the ball vanishes it has nothing to go on.

chapter 1

Read the play, and remember that possessions last

160 clues a coach would use: who is near the ball, who runs towards it, how fast it travels, whether it's near the line. A learning program works out which matter.

Then a smoother that knows football turns flickering guesses into clean blocks, charging a cost for every change of team.

Versions v1 and v1.1 · Source: experiments/RESULTADOS.md

We also trained on data deliberately damaged to look like TV tracking, so the system doesn't collapse when the ball goes missing.
chapter 2

Look around each moment, and at who controls the space

A model with memory reads seconds before and after, like rewinding the tape. A change detector answers one thing: is the ball changing hands right now?

And space control: which team could reach the ball first, not just who is nearest.

We froze this version as our safety net. Every later idea had to beat it.

Versions v2 to v4 · external match experiments

Space control helped most on the new-supplier test, which told us it generalises beyond the German matches.
chapter 3

Fix the impossible, and treat both teams the same

Teleporting ball: 40 metres in a tenth of a second is a camera error, not football. We spot those jumps and draw a sensible path.

Mirror test: every moment is judged twice, the second time flipped with shirts swapped, then averaged. This gives both teams the same role in the calculation.

Tracking repair and team symmetry · early system

We also practised with "blurry glasses": the training matches came from clean stadium cameras, so we damaged them on purpose until they looked like ours. The system learned to read how all 22 players move when the ball can't be seen.
chapter 4

Six different experts vote on every moment

Three snapshot experts study one moment in detail. Two film experts read seconds of play forwards and backwards. One shape expert sees the 22 players as a flock, ignoring shirt numbers.

They make different mistakes, so together they make fewer.

We also fixed fake stoppages: if Barça has the ball, the system briefly thinks it went out, and Barça still has it after, it was never a stoppage.
chapter 5

Four seconds with the ball in the hands

We lined up errors from the external matches around restarts. The camera can see a player holding the ball well before play resumes.

At a throw-in, the old system started the possession when the player picked the ball up, seconds too early. Now it waits for the ball to leave the hands.

Same idea elsewhere: a shin deflection isn't a steal, boundaries snap to the kick, and one-second interceptions are no longer smoothed away.

v8.5 restart analysis · schematic example

Every fix was checked on all four tests. Two of them first hurt the new-supplier test, so we added a safety check: they only act when the committee agrees.

What we tried and crossed out

Guess where the hidden ball is

It kept landing in midfield and invented possession changes. Guessing who has it works better than guessing where it is.

Missing players means a hidden duel

Players drop off the TV picture all the time. The model mistook gaps in coverage for changes of possession.

Trust the player list order

Each supplier orders players differently. We sort by distance to the ball instead.

Move every boundary to the nearest kick

Better on German matches, worse on the new supplier. Kept only with the committee's agreement.

Let the smoother learn team habits

It started favouring the team that usually has more of the ball. Changes now cost the same for both.

v9 · A touch or a turnover?

A defender gets a foot to a pass. The ball changes direction. Did the defender ever control it?

The segment model compares two accounts of the same play. A deflection keeps the original possession intact. A controlled interception opens a short spell for the other team. Evidence at both ends of the spell matters.

v10 · Who is moving to the ball?

Freeze the picture and two players can look equally close. Rewind a few steps and one is running away while the other is closing in.

The trajectory model follows each player through a short sequence. It relates those paths to the ball and estimates where control changes. The player-set model adds the shape of both teams.

v11 · Three views of a change

The physics model reads speed, acceleration and contact cues. The trajectory model follows movement. Trees supply another estimate from distances and angles.

The synthesis step combines their evidence while preserving the estimated share of dead ball within each half. A duration-aware decoder then finds a sequence of spells that fits the evidence.

The first touch after a stoppage

A restart tag gives us a time to look around. A whistle can help locate a stoppage. Neither alone tells us when a player first controls the ball.

At a throw-in, we follow the release and the first controlled touch. The segment stage can preserve a short possession between the restart and the next turnover.

What went into the entry

We submitted the retrained v11 grand variant. It turned the combined model signals into one home, away or dead-ball label per frame.

The export follows the organisers’ FAQ for the second-half kickoff. We checked the file format and recorded a SHA-256 fingerprint so we could identify the exact delivery.

What changed when we retrained

The original v11 weights were no longer available. We retrained locally, then compared the two versions on the same external matches before applying the kickoff correction.

The retrained version scored lower on both the Sportec and Metrica comparisons. The team chose a version it could replay from retained inputs.
